A second pair of eyes is what makes cash trustworthy. A lodgment is checked by an officer other than the one who lodged it.

## Before you start

You need the **verify cash lodgments** permission. You can't verify a lodgment you made yourself.

## Check a lodgment

<PlatformTabs>
<PlatformTab>
<Steps>
<Step>Go to <MenuPath items={["Finance", "Cash Collections", "Lodgments"]} />. **Waiting** lists lodgments to check.</Step>
<Step>Open one. Compare the amount, date and bank with the deposit slip and your bank statement.</Step>
<Step>Select **Verify lodgment** if it is on the statement, or **Reject** and give a reason if it isn't.</Step>
</Steps>
</PlatformTab>
<PlatformTab>
<Steps>
<Step>Go to <MenuPath items={["Manage", "Cash", "Lodgments"]} />. **Waiting** lists lodgments to check.</Step>
<Step>Open one. Compare the amount, date and bank with the slip (tap it to zoom) and your bank statement.</Step>
<Step>Tap **Verify lodgment** if it is on the statement, or **Reject** and give a reason if it isn't.</Step>
</Steps>
</PlatformTab>
</PlatformTabs>

Both decisions are final, and only one officer's decision counts if two try at once.

## What happens next

- **Verified** — the cash moves from *Cash in hand* to your bank account in the accounting records (if you use Accounting), and the officer is told.
- **Rejected** — the cash returns to the officer's hand with your reason, and they lodge it again.

## Lodged short

A lodgment marked **Lodged short** shows how much is missing and the reason given. Verifying it confirms the money that was paid in; the shortfall stays visible as cash not yet accounted for, so follow it up with the officer.
