If your association runs a cooperative, this is where you check your savings balance, apply for a loan, and respond when someone asks you to guarantee theirs.

## How it works

<Screenshot src="/images/member-guide/savings-and-loans-1.webp" alt="My Savings page showing an account's Current Balance, Total Deposits, Total Withdrawals and Interest Earned, with Deposit and Withdraw buttons" />

**My Savings** lists every savings account opened for you — an officer opens the account, and deposits and withdrawals both appear in its history. **My Loans** shows anything you've borrowed, or lets you apply for a new one if you have none active.

<Screenshot src="/images/member-guide/savings-and-loans-2.webp" alt="My Loans page with no active loans and an Apply for Loan button" />

### Apply for a loan

<Steps>
<Step>Go to <MenuPath items={["Cooperatives", "Apply"]} />.</Step>
<Step>Choose a **Loan Product** — its interest rate, tenure range and how many guarantors it needs are shown upfront.</Step>
<Step>Enter the **Amount** and **Tenure**, your **Purpose**, and name the required number of **Guarantors** from your member list.</Step>
<Step>Review and submit.</Step>
</Steps>

<Screenshot src="/images/member-guide/savings-and-loans-3.webp" alt="Apply for Loan form showing the Personal Loan product's amount range, interest rate, tenure and guarantor requirement" />

Your application then waits for your named guarantors to accept, before an officer reviews and disburses it.

### Respond to a guarantor request

If another member names you as a guarantor on their loan, it's meant to appear under <MenuPath items={["Cooperatives", "Guarantors"]} /> for you to accept or decline.

<Screenshot src="/images/member-guide/savings-and-loans-4-pending.webp" alt="Guarantor Requests page showing no pending requests" />

<Callout type="note" title="Still rolling out">
Guarantor requests don't reliably show up on this screen yet. If someone has named you as a guarantor, an officer can confirm your request's status in the meantime.
</Callout>

## Why it matters

Being a guarantor is a real commitment — accepting one means you share responsibility if the borrower can't repay — so being able to see and respond to a request yourself, rather than finding out some other way, matters.
