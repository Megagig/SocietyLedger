In a collector-thrift circle, most money starts as cash in a collector's hand. SocietyLedger never holds that cash. What it keeps is a record of **who gave what, who has it, and whether an officer has seen it**. Reconciliation is the step that turns a collector's record into confirmed money, and it is what makes the records trustworthy.

## The four steps

1. **Recorded.** The collector takes cash from a saver and records it in the app. The saver is told, and the payment shows as **Cash received, awaiting confirmation**. It is not counted as saved yet.
2. **Handed over.** At the end of the day the collector hands the day's cash to the office and records how much they handed over.
3. **Counted.** An officer counts the cash, and ticks each payment that was really in the bag.
4. **Confirmed.** Every ticked payment becomes **Confirmed**, is counted in the saver's savings and the circle's figures, and the saver gets a receipt.

Only step 4 changes anyone's savings. Until then the collector's record is a claim, and the officer's count is the check on it.

## What the officer sees

The handover compares three numbers:

- **Collected:** everything the collector recorded that day, plus any cash left over from an earlier day.
- **Handed over:** what the collector says they gave the office.
- **Difference:** handed over less collected. **Zero means they handed in all of it.**

The officer adds a fourth, what they **counted**. The count has to equal the payments they tick, to the naira. If it does not, SocietyLedger will not save it. This stops a count being typed in that does not match the payments being confirmed.

## A worked example

On Friday the collector records cash from five savers: **₦1,000.00, ₦1,000.00, ₦2,000.00, ₦1,000.00 and ₦2,000.00**. That is **₦7,000.00 collected**.

In the evening the collector hands over **₦6,000.00** and says Chukwuemeka's ₦1,000.00 is still with them, to bring in the morning.

The officer counts **₦6,000.00**, ticks the four payments that were in the bag, and leaves Chukwuemeka's unticked. The officer writes a note saying what happened, because the count differs from the payments recorded.

- The four ticked payments become Confirmed and get receipts.
- Chukwuemeka's ₦1,000.00 stays **Cash received, awaiting confirmation**. It is not lost and it is not counted.
- It is **carried over**: on Saturday it is added to what the collector collected, so Saturday's handover is ₦1,000.00 higher than Saturday's own cash. When the officer counts Saturday and ticks it, it is confirmed.

If the ₦1,000.00 never appears, the payment stays unconfirmed and shows in the officer's [accountability report](/docs/ajo-thrift/officer-accountability) as cash the office has not seen.

## Rules that keep it honest

- **The collector cannot confirm their own cash.** Someone else, with permission to reconcile, does the counting. A group with only one such person can confirm its own, but must write a note, and it is flagged.
- **A note is required** whenever a payment was not in the bag or the count differs from what the collector said they handed over.
- **A counted handover is final.** To correct a mistake, an officer reverses a payment with a reason. Nothing is edited or deleted.
- **Cash recorded after a handover** for that day is not allowed. The collector hands over once a day, at the end.
- **Cash is recorded online.** A collector's phone needs internet to record cash or hand it over. Nothing is saved for later, so a payment is never in the app twice or not at all.

## Bank transfers

A saver who pays by transfer follows a shorter path. They report the transfer with proof, and an officer checks it against the group's bank statement and confirms it. It is the same idea: a claim, then a check, then confirmed. See [Confirming payments](/docs/ajo-thrift/confirming-payments).

<Callout type="note" title="Why this matters to savers">
A saver sees exactly where their cash is: with the collector, handed to the office, or confirmed. It is the answer to "did my money get there?" without having to ask anyone.
</Callout>
