When a Finance transaction is synced, Accounting picks its debit and credit accounts automatically, based on the transaction's category type.

A synced transaction's category type decides which two accounts it touches:

| Category type | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Due | 1121 — Member Dues Receivable | 4110 — Membership Dues |
| Levy | 1122 — Levy Receivable | 4200 — Levy Income |
| Donation | 1112 — Bank Account - Operating | 4300 — Donation Income |
| Penalty | 1121 — Member Dues Receivable | 4400 — Penalty Income |

Worked example: a ₦5,000 due, once synced, posts as a debit of ₦5,000 to **Member Dues Receivable** and a credit of ₦5,000 to **Membership Dues** — the entry is created already **Posted**, skipping the draft/approval steps a manually-entered journal entry goes through.

<Callout type="note" title="Receivable, not cash, until a payment is recorded">
Syncing a transaction records what's *owed*, not cash received — it debits a receivable account. A separate payment against that transaction, once synced, is what moves the amount into a bank account.
</Callout>

## Why it matters

This is why a due can increase your income statement's revenue before a single naira actually arrives — the receivable side of the entry recognises the income once the charge exists, the same way the real README's own account-mapping table describes it.
