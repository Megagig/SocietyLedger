The cash flow statement tracks cash specifically — separate from income, which can include amounts still owed rather than collected.

## How it works

<Steps>
<Step>Go to <MenuPath items={["Accounting", "Cash Flow"]} />.</Step>
<Step>Pick a **Period**.</Step>
</Steps>

<Screenshot src="/images/accounting-guide/cash-flow-1.webp" alt="Cash Flow report with Operating, Investing and Financing sections and a beginning-to-ending cash summary" />

Worked example: the same ₦50,000 dues entry from [How charges and payments post to accounts](/docs/accounting-guide/how-transactions-post) only debited a **receivable** account, not a bank account — so it moves the income statement but not cash flow:

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Operating Activities — Net Cash | ₦0 |
| Investing Activities — Net Cash | ₦0 |
| Financing Activities — Net Cash | ₦0 |
| Beginning Cash Balance | ₦0 |
| **Ending Cash Balance** | **₦0** |

Cash only moves here once the matching **payment** is recorded and synced — a due being posted is a promise to pay, not cash in hand.

## Why it matters

This is the statement that catches the gap between "we recorded ₦1.2M in dues this year" and "we actually have ₦1.2M in the bank" — a healthy income statement with a flat cash flow statement usually means a lot of uncollected receivables.
