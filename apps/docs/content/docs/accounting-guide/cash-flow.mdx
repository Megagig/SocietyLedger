The cash flow statement tracks cash only. It is separate from income, which can include money still owed rather than collected.

## How it works

<PlatformTabs>
<PlatformTab>
<Steps>
<Step>Go to <MenuPath items={["Accounting", "Cash Flow"]} />.</Step>
<Step>Pick a **Period**.</Step>
</Steps>
</PlatformTab>
<PlatformTab>
<Steps>
<Step>Go to <MenuPath items={["Manage", "Accounting", "Financial statements"]} /> and choose **Cash flow**.</Step>
<Step>Pick a **Period**. Under the totals, each kind of cash movement lists what came in (**In:**) and what went out (**Out:**), with its net.</Step>
<Step>Tap the share icon for text, or the PDF icon (if you may export) for a PDF.</Step>
</Steps>
</PlatformTab>
</PlatformTabs>

Worked example: cash at the start was ₦1,000, you received ₦3,000 in dues and spent ₦800 on hall hire:

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Cash at the start | ₦1,000 |
| From running the association | ₦2,200 |
| From buying or selling assets | ₦0 |
| From loans and equity | ₦0 |
| **Cash at the end** | **₦3,200** |

A due being raised is a promise to pay, not cash. Cash moves here only once the payment is recorded.

## What it counts

- **Bank and cash accounts only.** These are asset accounts whose name or code marks them as cash or bank. A "Bank Charges" expense is not one.
- **Opening cash is what stood just before the period began,** so nothing on the first day is counted twice.
- **Moving money between two cash accounts is not an inflow or an outflow.** It is left out, since it nets to nothing.
- **It checks itself.** If the start plus the movements does not equal the end, the statement says so.

Select cash at the start or at the end to see the cash and bank accounts, and a thing that came in or went out to see its journal entry. See [Opening a figure to see the entries behind it](/docs/accounting-guide/opening-a-figure).
